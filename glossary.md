# Glossary

**acceptance criteria** — the concrete checks under a user
story that define "done." The test plan in disguise.

**backlog** — the ordered list of everything that might
get built. The product backlog is the wish list; the sprint
backlog is this sprint's commitment.

**burndown chart** — a graph of remaining work over a
sprint. Useful for the team; weaponized by bad managers.

**cadence** — the fixed rhythm of sprints. The regularity
is the point — it forces demos and retros on schedule.

**cargo-cult agile** — performing the ceremonies without
the thinking. Looks like agile from a distance; isn't.

**cycle time** — how long a Kanban item takes from started
to done. Shorter and predictable beats heroic and erratic.

**daily scrum** — the 15-minute daily synchronization:
what I did, what I'll do, what's blocking me. Not a status
meeting.

**definition of done** — the shared, written checklist a
piece of work must satisfy. Ends more arguments than any
framework.

**epic** — a large outcome too big to estimate, split into
stories. Months of work in a trench coat.

**increment** — the working, tested, potentially shippable
output of a sprint.

**INVEST** — Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable,
Small, Testable: the checklist for a good user story.

**Kanban** — visualize the workflow, limit work in
progress, manage flow. No sprints, no required roles.

**planning poker** — simultaneous-reveal team estimation.
The conversation it forces is the point.

**product owner** — the one person who owns the what, the
why, and the backlog order. One person, not a committee.

**retrospective** — the regular honest look at how the
team works, ending in one owned change. The highest-leverage
agile practice.

**Scrum** — sprints, three roles, four ceremonies, three
artifacts. The most common framework; incomplete by design.

**scrum master** — owns the process and removes blockers.
Not a manager, not a secretary.

**sprint** — a fixed-length iteration (1–4 weeks, usually
2) containing planning, work, review, and retrospective.

**sprint review** — the end-of-sprint demo of working
software to stakeholders. Demos, not slides.

**story points** — relative effort estimates (1, 2, 3, 5,
8, 13). Team-specific by design.

**throughput** — Kanban items finished per week. The honest
measure of capacity.

**user story** — a work item in user-voice form: "as a
[who], I want [what] so that [why]." A placeholder for a
conversation.

**velocity** — story points completed per sprint, averaged.
A forecasting tool; a terrible performance metric.

**waterfall** — the old sequential model: requirements,
design, build, test, in order. Works for bridges; fails
for software.

**WIP limit** — the maximum items allowed in a Kanban
column. The mechanism that forces finishing over starting.
