# Agile for Mere Mortals

**Agile** is a way of building software in small steps, checking
what you built, and adjusting — instead of planning everything
up front and hoping. This guide covers the **manifesto** behind
it, **Scrum** and **Kanban** (the two big frameworks), **user
stories**, **estimation**, how agile goes wrong in the real
world, and what parts are worth keeping when it's just you or
a tiny team.

No certifications required. Ideas first, then the practices
that actually earn their keep.

## What this guide covers

- [**What agile is**](what-is-agile.md) — the manifesto, the
  four values, and what agile is not
- [**Scrum**](scrum.md) — sprints, roles, and ceremonies:
  the most common framework, explained straight
- [**Kanban**](kanban.md) — visualizing flow and limiting
  work in progress: the quieter alternative
- [**User stories**](user-stories.md) — writing work items
  people can actually build from
- [**Planning and estimation**](planning-estimation.md) —
  story points, velocity, and why estimates are always wrong
- [**How agile goes wrong**](agile-failing.md) — cargo-cult
  agile, micromanagement theater, and other failure modes
- [**Agile for small teams**](agile-for-small-teams.md) —
  what to keep when it's just you or three people
- [**Troubleshooting**](troubleshooting.md) — symptom → fix,
  seven ways

## What it doesn't cover

Scaling frameworks (SAFe, LeSS), agile coaching as a career,
and the certification industry. This guide is about *doing
agile on a real team* — not selling it.

## How to use this guide

Agile is methodology, not tooling, so there are no commands
to run here. Claims about Scrum and Kanban were checked
against the Scrum Guide and standard Kanban references
before shipping. Framework details drift over the years;
anything version-sensitive says so.
