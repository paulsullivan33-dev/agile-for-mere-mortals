# Troubleshooting

Symptom → fix, in the order you'll hit them.

## Standups take 45 minutes

**Symptom:** the "15-minute" standup sprawls into problem-
solving and status theater.

**Cause:** no one enforces the format, or it's really a
status meeting for a manager.

**Fix:** the facilitator cuts discussion with "take it
offline after standup" — every time, kindly, until the
habit forms. If one person needs everyone's status, that's
a reporting need; solve it with a written update, not by
hijacking the team's synchronization.

## Retros change nothing

**Symptom:** the same complaints appear every retro; action
items die quietly.

**Cause:** retros produce wishes, not owned commitments.

**Fix:** each retro ends with *one* change, *one* owner,
and a check-in next retro ("did the change happen? did it
help?"). One change that sticks beats ten that don't.

## Everything is priority one

**Symptom:** the backlog has no order, or the top ten
items are all "urgent."

**Cause:** no one has the authority — or the stomach — to
say no.

**Fix:** that's the Product Owner's job, and it requires
backing from above. A practical forcing function: the team
may only work on the top N items, so ordering *is* the
decision. "Everything is urgent" collapses the moment
someone must pick what goes first.

## Estimates are always wrong (in the same direction)

**Symptom:** every sprint delivers half what was planned.

**Cause:** planning from wishes instead of measured
velocity, or pressure to commit to more.

**Fix:** plan the next sprint from the average of the last
three sprints' *completed* points — not from optimism.
If velocity is 20 and the plan needs 40, the plan is
wrong, not the team. Say so early; it's cheaper.

## The board is a lie

**Symptom:** the Kanban board says three items in progress;
developers are actually juggling nine.

**Cause:** the board doesn't match reality, so nobody
trusts it, so nobody updates it — a death spiral.

**Fix:** rebuild the board from reality in one session:
walk the actual work, place every item honestly, set WIP
limits you can keep. A board with an embarrassing "blocked"
column is infinitely more useful than a pretty fiction.

## "Agile" was imposed from above

**Symptom:** a reorg declares the company agile; teams get
new titles, same managers, same deadlines.

**Cause:** process adopted as rebranding, without the
authority shifts agile requires.

**Fix:** you can't fix this from inside a ceremony. The
honest moves: find the smallest unit where you *can* run
the real loop (one team, real backlog, real demos) and
prove it works there — or recognize the label is
permanent and stop expecting the substance.

## One person does all the agile

**Symptom:** the Scrum Master maintains the board, writes
the stories, runs the retros — the team just codes.

**Cause:** agile became one person's job instead of the
team's way of working.

**Fix:** rotate facilitation. The team owns the process
together or it isn't theirs. A Scrum Master who does
everything *for* the team is a very organized bottleneck.
